Public Transport Usage in Australia

How Australians Ride Trains, Trams, Buses and Ferries.

Public transport patronage means the number of trips people make on trains, trams (also called light rail), buses and ferries. Australia's big cities had been adding riders every year until the pandemic emptied them almost overnight.

Five years later, public transport recovery remains uneven across Australian cities. This page examines the national recovery, takes a closer look at Sydney and Melbourne, and explores how cheaper fares helped bring passengers back.

1 The Big Picture — How far has public transport recovered across Australia's eight capital cities since the pandemic?

Trips across the capitals fell to 862 million in 2021–2022, less than half their usual level. By 2024–2025 Perth had passed its old record, Brisbane and Canberra were back to normal, but Melbourne was still 18% short.

Patronage

One trip or boarding on a train, tram, bus or ferry. A journey with a change of vehicle can count as two.

Financial year

Australia's government year runs July to June, so "2024–2025" means July 2024 to June 2025.

2 Sydney: Recovery — How quickly did Sydney's riders return, and where did they go once the new Metro opened?

Sydney recorded just 9 million trips in August 2021, down from 63 million in a typical 2019 month. By 2025, ridership had recovered to 88%, with the new Metro shifting passenger flows to different stations.

Riders moved, they didn't vanish

Town Hall fell from 71 million trips in 2019 to 41 million in 2025, and Wynyard from 49 million to 27 million. Yet Gadigal — a new Metro station a block from Town Hall — carried 11.7 million trips in its first full year, and Metro as a whole carried 72 million. Part of the "lost" city-centre traffic simply moved underground. Meanwhile the Bankstown line stations dropped to almost nothing because the line closed in 2024 to be rebuilt as Metro.

3 Melbourne: The City-Centre Effect — Why Melbourne's city-centre stations recover slower than its suburbs?

Victoria's trains, trams and buses carried 477 million trips in 2025, 81% of 2019. Its station data shows why the city centre matters. At Flinders Street, Southern Cross and Melbourne Central, only 6–10% of peak-time boardings happen in the morning.

These are places people travel to for work — so when office workers stay home, these stations lose the most.

4 What brings riders back: The Price of a Ticket

On 5 August 2024, Queensland cut every public transport fare in the south-east to just 50 Australian cents. In the same December-to-July months a year later, South East Queensland carried 16% more trips and the gain held into a second year.

Victoria's summer of free weekend travel, by contrast, barely moved its monthly totals.

Every mode carried more people after fares fell to 50 cents. Overall, average monthly trips rose 16%, from 13.7 million to 16.0 million.

  • Who rode more: ferry and light rail grew fastest, which suggests cheap fares attracted optional trips, such as weekend outings, not only commuting.
  • Not proof of cause: The charts show changes following the fare cut. However, this does not mean the fare cut was the only cause. Other factors like population growth, may also have contributed to the increase in ridership.
  • Victoria's special case: its free travel (chart below) covered weekends only, so it barely shows in monthly totals. Its jump from March 2026 came after the offer ended.

Change in Monthly Trips from a Year Earlier: Queensland and Victoria

Queensland: cheap fares lifted travel, and the lift has held.

  • January – July 2025: +16% on average. Each month is compared with the same month on full fares, so this shows the effect of the fare cut.
  • From August 2025: +11% on average. Both years now had 50-cent fares, so this is growth on top of the cut. Leaving out March 2026, which is compared with the cyclone-hit March 2025, it is about +8%.

Victoria: free weekend travel made no visible difference.

  • During the offer (December 2025 – January 2026): about 0%. Trips were almost the same as a year earlier (+0.1% and −0.2%).
  • April 2026: +25%, but this came after the offer had ended, so it is not a free-travel effect.
  • Monthly totals mix weekday and weekend trips, so a small weekend-only effect could be hidden.
